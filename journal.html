Duckdoku: building a game in public

A puzzle game where you place one duck in every color, row and column, and ducks can't touch. I am building it from a single idea to a polished game, and I am writing down every step.

Day 1: From a chat to a plan

The idea

I love the cat puzzle game Meowdoku by Oakever Games. I wanted to know: can I build my own version, with my own theme and my own art? I asked ChatGPT.

The answer was: copy the mechanics (the rules), not the assets (art, sounds, levels). So I chose ducks instead of cats, and the name Duckdoku.

I kept four ChatGPT chats, in order. They are my design notes:

  1. Clone game mechanics. Rules, puzzle generator, screens, ads, coins, streaks.
  2. Player Income per Minute (IPM). A way to set every price and reward from "how many minutes of play is this worth?"
  3. Hints and the name. Three kinds of hints. Names like Capydoku, Foxdoku... I chose Duckdoku.
  4. The duck helper. A duck button that flies onto the board and places one correct duck.

The rules of Duckdoku

  • The board has colored areas. Every color must get exactly one duck.
  • One duck per row and one per column.
  • Ducks cannot touch, not even on the corner.
  • You have 3 hearts. A wrong duck costs a heart.
  • Tap a cell once to cross it out. Double-tap to place a duck.

My reference screenshots

I took 23 screenshots of Meowdoku: the splash, the tutorial, the grid, the hint bar, the profile, the daily streak and the "Bravo" win screen. They are my style guide. I replace every cat with a duck.

One thing I checked before building: the shared chat and my screenshots did not match on the animal (chat: ducks, screenshots: cats). That is expected. The screenshots are what I copy, the chat is what I build. I also noticed that the chat says the cat is "2D with 3D-style parts", while I see it as a 3D cat. So I made the duck a 3D-looking picture inside a 2D game.

The 3D duck idea

In the reference, the cat looks to the top-left or top-right, and sometimes winks at you. It also winks when you place it on the grid. My game is 2D, but my duck is a 3D render. To get this feeling, I swap between rendered poses:

straight -> look top-left / top-right -> back -> (sometimes) wink

Day 1: The puzzle engine

I built the brain of the game before any screens. Three parts:

  1. Generator. It places ducks so no two touch, grows colored areas around them, then checks the puzzle has exactly one answer. If not, it tries again. The same level number always gives the same puzzle.
  2. Solver. Counts solutions, up to 2. This is how I know a puzzle is fair.
  3. Board. Holds the state: crosses, ducks, hearts, score, undo, hints, helper.

Hints

The hint bulb finds a real deduction and says it in words, like "This color has only one place left!" Then it glows the color and shows a hand on the cell.

Money (from my IPM chat)

Reward = expected solve minutes x income per minute.

4x4 = 100 coins/min, and it grows with board size. A hint or a helper duck costs 100 coins once free ones run out. You get 3 free helper ducks per day.

Day 3: Feedback round. Twelve things to fix and add

After playing the first version, I wrote down 12 problems and wishes. Here is what I did about each one.

1. The "I cannot place a duck" bug (the most important one)

What was wrong: I trusted Unity's own "click count" to know if I double-tapped. The input system only counts two clicks if they are very fast (about 0.3 seconds), so on a real mouse or finger the game almost never saw a double-tap. The duck could not be placed.

The fix: I now measure the double-tap myself. Two taps on the same cell within 0.45 seconds place a duck. The first tap's cross is taken back automatically. This does not depend on the input system any more.

2. Buttons like the reference (image 1)

All text buttons are now flat pills with round ends, wide and short: orange for the main action, grey when locked, white for the second choice, green for extras. Level 1 and a locked Daily Challenge (opens after 5 levels) copy the reference idea. I kept the round icon buttons round.

3. Copy the style, not every pixel

Day 4: More feedback. 17 changes

I played the game again and wrote down 17 things. Here is what changed.

The double-tap bug, again (the real cause)

Last time I measured the double-tap myself, but I was still listening for Unity's click event. A click is thrown away if the finger moves even a little between press and release. That is why it worked "sometimes". Now the game listens for the press itself, so nothing is thrown away, and the time window is 1 second (I had 0.45 seconds).

New: drag to cross many cells

Press a cell and drag over other cells: they all get a cross. If you start on a cell that already has a cross, dragging takes the crosses away.

The puzzle plan (from the design chat)

The design chat said the game must teach the rules through play, and that every puzzle needs exactly one answer and a difficulty a person can reach without guessing. I built that in three parts.

1. A level table (LevelDesign)

The starting ducks have a small lock and cannot be moved. The rule the level teaches is lit up with an orange border on the rule card, and the coach says it in one line.

2. A "human solver" (HumanSolver). It solves each puzzle like a person, and counts how hard the deductions were:

  1. only one place is left in a row, column or color
  2. a color fits in one row or column only
  3. the same with two colors
  4. "what if I put a duck here?" ends in a dead end, or the same with three colors

If none of these help, the puzzle needs guessing, and it is thrown away.

3. A generator that keeps only puzzles that fit the level. Same level, same puzzle, every time. Each puzzle shows a word under the title: Very easy, Easy, Medium, Hard, Expert.

Test result, all 200 levels:

  • Every level has exactly one solution.
  • Every level fits its row in the table (size, ducks, difficulty). 0 misses.
  • Speed: 1 ms on average, 20 ms at worst.
  • Difficulty goes up from 1.0/10 at level 1 to about 8/10 at level 200 (levels 1-100 are easy to medium).

My first version failed on the big boards (levels 101 and up): puzzles with one answer were too rare on 8x8. The fix: when a puzzle has several answers, the generator nudges single cells to another color until only one answer is left.

Economy numbers changed to match the plan

  • Reward = expected minutes x income of the world (100, 110, 120, 135 ... 270 per minute, one world per 20 levels). Level 1 = 100 coins, level 5 = 150, level 10 = 200.
  • Helper duck: 150 coins (was 100).

I first built the level table so that levels 1-20 each taught one rule (rows, then columns...). You said no: every level uses all the rules, and the tutorial (level zero) teaches them. I removed the lesson text and the orange rule highlight. The table keeps only the board size, the number of starting ducks, and the difficulty.

Day 7: Breathing room, a better shop button, and the first Android build

Spacing (checked on every screen)

Rule I now follow: at least 40 units of empty space around anything inside a card, and about 100 units between the bottom button row and the edge of the phone. The old game screen had its bottom captions only 13 units from the edge, which a phone's gesture bar would cover.

  • Game screen: re-flowed from top to bottom. The board is a bit smaller (860 instead of 900) to give every row more space. The bottom buttons sit higher.
  • Complete and Failed cards: taller, so the last button has real space under it.
  • Shop: the cards are taller (700), every card has the same layout (picture, name, price, button), and both the Ducks and Outfits tabs scroll. The "Coming soon" card is taller and its button no longer touches the edge.
  • Settings, Profile, Restart popup, tutorial end buttons: more space between the buttons and from the edge.

Coins while playing

The game screen (and the daily challenge, which uses the same screen) now has a coin chip next to hearts and score. When you pay for something, the number counts down and a red -150 floats away, so it is clear where the coins went.

Shop button

It is now a big orange button with the treasure chest sticking out on the left, and a red ! when there is something you can afford.

Test page fixes

  • Buttons in the test page no longer play the button click.
  • Two buttons no longer light up together: the page remembers which button you pressed, not which sound file (Wrong duck and Failed use the same file, and so do Coin arrives and Cross out).

Android

Package name com.sid.duckdoku, ARM64, portrait, IL2CPP, minimum Android 8 (API 26). The 5 GB sound packs are not in the build (only the small Resources folder is), and the Sound Lab is editor-only.

Day 8: Bigger back buttons, a full-screen "life lost", ads, purchases and the Play Store

Look and feel

  • Streak page: the "This week" card is taller, the title has space around it, and the day chips and gift pictures are bigger.
  • Game screen: on tall phones the extra height now goes into bigger rows and more space between them (hearts, score, coins, ducks row, rule cards).
  • Back and settings buttons are bigger (150) and sit further from the screen edge on the game, home, streak and shop screens.
  • Life lost: every lost heart now plays a full-screen effect: red flash, screen shake, a broken heart and a sad duck with "-1 heart" and "2 hearts left". It does not block play. On the last heart it plays first, then the FAILED card comes.
  • Settings no longer shows the old prototype text.

Money side (nothing was live yet, all tested with test ads and fake purchases)

  • Rewarded ads (the player chooses to watch): free hint, free helper duck, free coins in the shop (+300), double coins on the Complete card, and a free heart on the Failed card.
  • Full-screen ads only after finishing a level, not before level 8, at most once every 5 levels and not twice within 5 minutes. No banner ads at all.
  • Consent form (Google's own) for players in the EU and UK, and a "Privacy policy" button in Settings.
  • Purchases (Google Play): remove_ads (one time), and three coin packs coins_small (2,000), coins_medium (6,000), coins_large (15,000). The shop has new tabs Coins and No ads (with "Restore purchases").
  • Why Unity In-App Purchasing if we use Google Play billing? It is only a helper library. Under the hood it talks to Google Play Billing, and it adds the BILLING permission Play Console asks for.
  • In the Unity Editor, ads are a fake 2-second screen and purchases are faked, so I can test without a phone.
